Like the title says, I trashed the first website I ever made. I was super excited to make something people would actually see, so I did a rush job and figured it'd be fine. A few months and a few projects later, I went back to update it. Then BOOM. I looked at it and was disgusted. I was genuinely horrified that at one point in my life I thought this was acceptable work. It looked like AI slop, the colors were a terrible design, and it wasn't mobile friendly. The weird part wasn't how bad it was. It was that I couldn't see any of it when I made it. Some flaws only show up after you put the thing out there.

While I was building it, I couldn't see a single flaw. I think the artist slowly goes blind to the art. No wonder people tell you to get a fresh pair of eyes on it. Time did that for me: a few months away and I was looking at it like a stranger would. I'm sure in a few months this new site will start showing its flaws too. But I think that's kind of the point. Iteration is the only way to move forward. There has to be a balance between trying to get the perfect website out and getting something out in the first place. It's the same balance as loving what you made while still being critical enough to spot what's wrong with it.

I actually learned this when I was 5 or 6. I loved building flying ships out of Legos, no instructions, straight off the top of my head. Version 1 of one ship was super big and super flimsy LMAO. Once it was done, obviously I had to play with it, so I lifted it up and it snapped in half from its own weight and hit the floor. I'd spent the whole weekend on it, and I was crushed. I remember my mom coming over, holding my hands, and telling me, “Don't worry, everything breaks because nothing is perfect, but the best thing you can do from here is build it again.” I genuinely hated that answer. What do you mean build it again? I didn't want to do all that over just for it to break again. That's when she hit me with the banger: “But now when you build it, you're gonna know what to change so it won't break again.” I didn't believe her. But when I built v2, it was a little ugly, and it didn't break.

That's how I think about the website now. The first one was my v1 ship: rushed, overbuilt, and it fell apart the moment I picked it back up. Throwing it away didn't waste the work. It's how I knew what to change this time. I added a mobile-friendly version and a better color theme, and made the whole thing feel more pieced together. The new site probably has flaws I can't see yet. When I find them, I'll build it again.
